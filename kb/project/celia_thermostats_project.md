## Overview

CELIA is a collection of aluminium heaters with integrated smart thermostats, designed by Andrei as part of Leroy Merlin's Innovation Team in Lille, France. The line shipped to Leroy Merlin stores and became best-sellers for three consecutive years. The thermostat interface — a monochrome liquid crystal screen with physical buttons — represents one of Andrei's earliest transitions from physical object design to digital interface design.

## Context and problem

Andrei spent a year in Leroy Merlin's Innovation Team in Lille, contributing to multiple projects including electrical plugs for factory workers, exterior plastic chairs, and a reusable box system for store buyers — all of which went into production and were sold in stores. CELIA was the primary focus: a new collection of heaters that needed to combine premium build quality with an integrated smart thermostat system.

## Role and scope

Andrei led the industrial design for the entire CELIA line:

- **Heater form and detailing**: Defined the shape and proportions of the aluminium heater bodies, spending weeks in the production factory in Germany perfecting the design.
- **Premium variant**: Designed a higher-end version where the thermostat is visually integrated into one of the vertical elements of the heater.
- **Smart thermostat interface**: Designed the interface for the monochrome liquid crystal screen with physical buttons below — bridging physical product design and digital interface design within the same project.
- **Factory collaboration**: Worked directly with the German production facility on material finishing and manufacturing feasibility.

## What was built

- **Aluminium heater collection**: Multiple heater variants produced in a factory in Germany, with a base version at 250 EUR and a premium version at 400 EUR.
- **Integrated smart thermostat**: A thermostat module with a monochrome liquid crystal display and physical button controls, connecting to the heaters.
- **Premium thermostat integration**: A higher-end variant where the thermostat is visually embedded into the heater's vertical structure rather than attached as a separate module.

## Outcomes

The CELIA collection shipped to Leroy Merlin stores and became best-sellers for three consecutive years. The experience designing the thermostat's monochrome LCD interface directly informed Andrei's subsequent work at Vector Watch, which initially used the same display technology.

## What this project represents

CELIA marks the point where Andrei's practice began crossing from pure industrial design into digital interface work. Designing the thermostat screen — within the constraints of monochrome liquid crystals and physical buttons — was the first step toward the wearable OS and mobile app work that followed at Vector Watch and Fitbit. The project also demonstrates direct factory collaboration: spending weeks on the production floor shaping the final product, a hands-on relationship with manufacturing that echoes in FRAM's current approach of working directly with the material.
