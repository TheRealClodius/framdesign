## Overview

Clara Lamp is a flat-pack geometric lamp made from laser-cut wood panels, designed as a university project at Politecnico di Milano. The lamp uses interlocking mechanical joints — no glue or adhesive — allowing users to assemble the octagonal form from flat-packed pieces. Andrei designed it alongside university teammate Simone Gasperi. The project is part of the Clara series, a name Andrei gave to projects from this period, named after close friends.

## Context and problem

The project brief required the use of laser cutting techniques to create connectable panels that form a three-dimensional shape. Andrei and Simone Gasperi wanted to push further by eliminating adhesive entirely, designing edge profiles that connect purely through mechanical interlocking. The additional constraint was to make the lamp deliverable as a flat pack with fast, intuitive assembly.

## Role and scope

Andrei led the design of the lamp form, the mechanical joint system, and the flat-pack delivery concept. He also directed the photography, including long-exposure studies capturing the lamp's light behavior and assembly documentation featuring glass artist Mihai Topescu assembling the lamp on top of Escape, a separate furniture piece made by Andrei.

## What was built

- **Laser-cut wood panels**: Geometric pieces with a custom edge profile designed for tool-free mechanical assembly.
- **Octagonal lamp form**: A geometric shape built from interlocking flat panels, housing an LED light source.
- **Flat-pack delivery concept**: The lamp ships as flat panels and assembles without adhesive or tools.
- **Photography series**: Assembly documentation and long-exposure studies showing the lamp's light behavior and physical presence.

## Outcomes

The lamp remained as a university prototype at Politecnico di Milano. It was not produced or exhibited beyond the academic context.

## What this project represents

Clara Lamp is an early exploration of constraint-driven design — starting from a manufacturing technique (laser cutting) and pushing it toward a purely mechanical assembly system. It demonstrates Andrei's interest in how production constraints shape form and in making the assembly process part of the user experience. The project is part of the Clara series alongside Clara Sofa.
